# Simulation Prep & Study Guide

Your complete roadmap for prepping the Financial Crisis role-play simulation. Read this **once before starting**, then skim sections 1 & 2 again right before the simulation.

---

## Overview: What You're Being Evaluated On

**As Media/Press:**
1. **Interviews during the live press conference** — questions that sound reactive but are actually coordinated, catch contradictions, reference the actual toolkit
2. **Press reports** — written copy tracking how the crisis unfolds (quality of reporting, not technical depth)

**What separates good press from great press:**
- Listen hard to what's actually said, then cross-question contradictions in real time
- Ask questions that force daylight between the central bank and government positions
- Reference the IMF toolkit strategically by name (RFI vs SBA vs EFF)
- Don't over-prepare; let the live statement guide your follow-ups

## Section 2: Press Strategy — How to Win

### A. Divide Labor Among the Press Group

**Before the crisis scenario is revealed** (in your WhatsApp press group), assign angles:

1. **Reserves / Currency numbers person** — how many billions burned, when do we hit the red line, what's the sustainability math
2. **Accountability person** — "did you see this coming, why didn't you act sooner, what were the warning signs you ignored"
3. **Political fallout person** — will there be resignations, is someone in the cabinet getting replaced, will there be political consequences
4. **Citizen impact person** — "what does this mean for a saver tomorrow, will my deposits be safe, what's the impact on ordinary people"

This ensures 3-4 press members ask *different* things instead of overlapping — it reads like a real newsroom dividing a story.

### B. Nominate the Cross-Questioner

**The single best move:** Pick one person whose entire job is to **listen hard and catch contradictions in the central bank's opening statement**, then follow up on them live. 

Examples of what to catch:
- "You just said reserves are adequate, but you also said intervention continues daily — which is it?"
- "You said the rupee is stabilizing, but you're also hiking rates to defend it — which direction are you trying to move?"
- "You announced a bailout for bank X, but also said no taxpayer funds were used — how does that work?"

This is what impressed Prof. Saggar most in a prior batch's run — it looked like genuine listening, not a pre-written monologue.

### C. Pre-Load Crisis-Agnostic Question Templates

Once the actual crisis scenario is revealed (~5 mins before the press conference), adapt these templates on the fly:

**Template 1: Challenge the assumption**
- "You've announced [action]. Why not [more aggressive alternative]? Is that being held in reserve because things are worse than stated?"

**Template 2: Push on the IMF question**
- "Has the government approached the IMF? If not yet, at what point does the delay itself become the story?"

**Template 3: Demand accountability**
- "Who takes the blame if this doesn't work — was this foreseeable? Which specific warning indicators did you track?"

**Template 4: Force daylight between stakeholders**
- "The Finance Minister said X, but you just said Y — which of you is right?" (This is the Saggar-approved move for realistic criticism.)

**Template 5: The IMF facility gotcha**
- "Are we heading toward an RFI (emergency, no time for negotiation) or a full SBA program (negotiated, conditional)? Or are you downplaying severity while quietly negotiating an FCL?"

### D. Target Each Role's Pressure Point

Know who you're asking. Each role in the room has something to hide:

| Who | Pressure Point | Killer Question |
|---|---|---|
| **RBI Governor** | Central bank independence | "Is your independence being compromised by government pressure to act faster/slower?" |
| **RBI DG (Monetary Policy)** | Growth vs currency defense | "Are you raising rates to defend the currency at the cost of growth?" |
| **RBI DG (Financial Markets)** | Reserves depleting | "How many billions in reserves have you burned this week, and what's the red line before you stop?" |
| **RBI DG (Regulation & Supervision)** | Bank health / bailout risk | "Which banks are under watch right now, and is a bailout being prepared?" |
| **Finance Minister / Secretary** | Political optics + IMF delay | "Is the government approaching the IMF, and if so, why the delay?" (Delay itself is an evaluation point.) |
| **IMF Mission Chief** | Conditionality terms | "What conditionalities are non-negotiable, and will there be austerity measures?" |
| **IMF ED India** | India's seat at IMF | "Is India's IMF voice big enough to shape these terms, or are you a rubber stamp?" |
| **Fed Chair** | Spillover responsibility | "Is US monetary tightening the real cause of this crisis, and do you bear responsibility for the spillover?" |

### E. Execution During the Press Conference

1. **Central bank announces first** — your job is to listen to what they *actually* said
2. **Ask your pre-planned questions**, but reframe them on the fly to address what was just stated, not what you expected
3. **Support the cross-questioner** if they've landed a contradiction — follow it up with a related angle
4. **Stay sharp on the IMF facility question** — naming the right instrument (RFI vs SBA vs EFF) signals you understand severity/timeline
5. **Don't over-promise a narrative** — journalism is adaptive, not a monologue

## Section 3: Crisis Identification Cheat Sheet

Use this to figure out what you're dealing with in the first 2-3 minutes after the scenario drops.

| Crisis Type | Trigger | What You'll See | Endgame | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Currency Crisis** | Exchange rate misalignment, capital flight, reserve depletion | Rupee/peso in free fall, RBI burning FX reserves defending it | Peg abandoned or IMF called in | Argentina, Taper Tantrum, ERM |
| **Banking Crisis** | Panic, bank run, NPAs | Depositors queuing, a bank/NBFC failing | LOLR support, bailout, nationalization | Asian Financial Crisis |
| **Sovereign Debt Crisis** | Unsustainable public debt | Bond yields spike, downgrade, can't roll over debt | Restructuring, haircut, IMF programme | Argentina (again), Greece |

**Your move:** Once you identify which type, you know which Tier 2 lecture you should have skimmed.

## Section 4: IMF Facility Cheat Sheet

Which facility is chosen is itself a story. Use this to ask the right follow-up questions.

| Situation | Facility | Meaning | Media Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Emergency, need cash fast, no time to negotiate** | **RFI** (Rapid Financing Instrument) | Outright disbursement, no conditionality reviews | "Things are bad and urgent — no time for a negotiated program" |
| **Ongoing BoP problem, standard case** | **SBA** (Stand-By Arrangement) | Up to 3 yrs, ex-post conditionality — the "default" IMF loan | "This is negotiated and conditional — watch for austerity backlash" |
| **Deep structural problem, not just a cash crunch** | **EFF** (Extended Fund Facility) | Up to 4 yrs, tied to structural reforms | "Long-term structural reform condition — expensive politically" |
| **Country not in crisis, wants insurance** | **FCL** (Flexible Credit Line) | Ex-ante qualification only, for strong-fundamentals countries | "We're fine, just a signal" — good gotcha if govt is downplaying severity while negotiating one |
| **Poor-country equivalents** | **ECF / SCF / RCF** | Concessional versions (PRGT-funded) of EFF/SBA/RFI | Same logic, different cost structure |

## Section 5: Vocabulary to Drop Naturally

These terms signal you understand the game without needing to do the math. Drop them in questions:

**Balance of Payments & External Sector:**
- BoP crisis, capital flight, sudden stop, original sin (EM can't borrow in own currency), twin deficits, fragile five

**IMF & Crisis Management:**
- Letter of Intent (LoI), conditionality, haircut, restructuring, LOLR (Lender of Last Resort), stigma (fear of IMF borrowing), moral hazard

**Financial Stress Signals:**
- Contagion, sovereign spread, credit rating downgrade, YieldCurve inversion

**RBI Toolkit:**
- Repo rate, CRR/SLR, standing liquidity facility, OMO (Open Market Operations), LTRO/TLRO, forward guidance, UMPTs (Unconventional Monetary Policy Tools)

## Section 6: Quick Reference — What Prof. Saggar Looks For

From his feedback on a prior batch:

- **Sharp, coordinated-but-spontaneous attack** — you plan together but execute like you're reacting live to what was just said
- **Clear listening** — you reference specific things said in the opening statement, not a pre-written monologue
- **Cross-questioning contradictions** — if the RBI Governor says X and the Finance Minister says Y, that's your story
- **Realistic tone** — sounds like actual press questioning (skeptical, following up) not a debate script
- **Vocabulary + specificity** — drop the right terms and ask for concrete numbers (reserves burned, austerity measures named, etc.)

The opposition's coordinated attack worked because it **felt real**, even though it was planned. You can do the same.
