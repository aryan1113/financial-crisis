## What Prof. Saggar specifically praised in a prior batch

In an earlier role-play run where the entire class was the press, one opposition party member attacked the central bank and finance minister. Prof. Saggar was delighted — he noted it **felt very real to him, even though it was pre-planned/staged**, because:
- The attack was sharp and coordinated but sounded spontaneous

**Key insight for the friend's press group prep:** plan your questions and divide your labor in advance, but execute them to *sound like* you're reacting live to what the central bank just said.

## The friend's specific role: Media / Press

### What they're being evaluated on

1. **Press reports** — written copy tracking how the crisis/negotiation unfolds (quality of reporting, not technical depth)
2. **Interviews** — asking questions during the live press conference that:
   - Sound reactive but are actually coordinated with the rest of the press group
   - Catch contradictions or evasions from the central bank's opening statement
   - Divide labor smartly (different angles: reserves, accountability, political fallout, citizen impact)
   - Reference the actual IMF/RBI toolkit and real-world precedent (Argentina, Asian Financial Crisis, Taper Tantrum)

### What separates good press from great press in this format

- **One person should listen for and cross-question a live contradiction** — if the RBI Governor says "reserves are adequate" but also "we're intervening daily," that's gold
- **Ask questions that force daylight between the central bank's and the government's position** — "The Finance Minister said X, but you just said Y — which of you is right?"
- **Reference the IMF toolkit strategically** — "Is this heading toward an RFI (emergency, no time for negotiation) or a full SBA program (negotiated, conditional)?" differentiates by severity
- **Don't over-prepare; let listening to the real statement guide your follow-ups** — the best journalism is adaptive, not a monologue
- **Drop the right vocabulary naturally** to signal competence without needing to understand the math** — capital flight, contagion, sovereign spread, conditionality, haircut, original sin, stigma, sudden stop, LoI, LOLR

### Prep steps for the friend

1. **Read Tier 1 from `reading_plan.md`** (Lec-13 Crisis Communication, Lec-05 IMF, Lec-17 Early Warning Indicators, Lec-03 Aggregates, Lec-02 Monetary Policy) — ~2 hours
2. **Skim `cheat_sheet.md`** before the simulation — 5 minutes
3. **In the WhatsApp press group** (before instructions drop):
   - Propose dividing labor: one person on reserves/numbers, one on accountability, one on political fallout, one on citizen impact
   - Nominate someone to be the "live cross-questioner" (listens hard, catches contradictions mid-statement, follows up immediately)
   - Agree on a few generic-but-adaptable question templates (see cheat sheet):
     - "Has the government approached the IMF? If not, at what point does delay become the story?"
     - "You said X, but you also said Y — which is it?"
     - "Why not [more aggressive alternative]? Is that being held in reserve because things are worse than stated?"
     - "Is this the government's fault, and what would you have done differently?" (for opposition angle)
4. **During the press conference**:
   - Central bank announces first
   - **Listen carefully** to what they actually said — this is your data
   - Ask your pre-planned questions, but reframe them on the fly to address what was just stated, not what you expected to hear
   - Support the cross-questioner if they've landed a contradiction — follow it up with a related angle
   - Stay sharp on the IMF-facility question — naming the right instrument (RFI vs. SBA vs. EFF) signals you understand severity/timeline
