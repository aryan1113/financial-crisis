# Select Financial Crises: 1920s–1990s (Great Depression, LatAm Debt, ERM, Tequila Crisis)

## Key Concepts

- **Great Depression (1929–39)**: Triggered by the 1920s "Roaring Twenties" stock bubble bursting on Black Thursday/Monday/Tuesday (Oct 24/28/29, 1929); Dow fell ~90% by 1932. Industrial production, prices, and trade collapsed across US/UK/France/Germany; US unemployment hit 24.9%.
  - *Friedman & Schwartz*: blamed the Fed for four policy mistakes (tightening in 1928, raising rates in 1931 to defend the dollar, another hike in late 1932, and neglecting bank failures/LOLR duty) that caused a monetary contraction.
  - *Eichengreen*: argues the Fed's hands were tied by the **gold standard**, which forced rate hikes to defend gold parity even as the economy needed easing — a lesson later echoed in the euro/2008-09 crisis debate.
- **Latin American Debt Crisis (1980s)**: Oil-shock surpluses were recycled via US banks into LatAm loans; debt ballooned from $29bn (1970) to $327bn. Volcker's 1979-80 anti-inflation rate hikes (Fed funds to 20%) triggered capital flight from LatAm and Mexico's 1982 default. Resolved via the 1989 **Brady Plan** (debt-for-bond restructuring, $60bn forgiven).
  - **Heterodox shock programs** (Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil): price/wage freezes + currency changes to break "inertial," indexed inflation. Brazil's Cruzado/Bresser/Summer/Collor Plans repeatedly failed; the 1994 **Plano Real** (URV transitional unit, gradual de-indexation) finally tamed hyperinflation, but fiscal slippage led to the 1999 "Samba Effect" currency crisis (dirty float overvaluation + Russian/Asian contagion + defensive rate hikes).
  - **Brady Bonds**: 25-30yr USD bonds collateralized by zero-coupon US T-bonds, restoring EM market access.
- **European ERM Crisis (1992-93)**: Fixed-peg system (ERM I) under Maastricht convergence criteria (inflation, deficit/debt, exchange-rate stability, interest rates) collapsed under speculative attack. **Black Wednesday** (16 Sept 1992): Soros's Quantum Fund shorted sterling; BoE's FX intervention failed and UK exited ERM. Lira, peseta, escudo, and other currencies also fell under pressure.
- **Mexican Tequila Crisis (1994-95)**: Peso peg defended via dollar-linked Tesabonos; political shocks (Chiapas uprising, Colosio assassination) raised risk premia; reserves drained defending the peg; Dec 1994 devaluation and float triggered capital flight, 52% inflation, and a $50bn US/IMF bailout. Illustrates **"original sin"** — EMs' inability to borrow abroad in domestic currency.

## Why This Matters

Primarily **conceptual/narrative** with light quantitative detail (some macro tables, rate/inflation figures) rather than derivations or models. Someone with only basic macroeconomics (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, forex reserves) would find this **accessible** — the core mechanisms (fixed pegs breaking under speculative pressure, reserve depletion, monetary tightening causing capital flight) map directly onto those fundamentals, though terms like "second-generation speculative attack models" or "original sin" need brief unpacking.

## Relevance to the Crisis Simulation

**High.** The Tequila crisis (peg defense, reserve depletion, IMF bailout) and ERM crisis (central bank vs. speculators, currency defense failure) are near-direct templates for a role-play involving RBI Governor/Deputies, Finance Ministry, IMF Mission Chief, and Fed Chair — providing concrete playbooks for both the crisis mechanics and the institutional decision points each role would face.
