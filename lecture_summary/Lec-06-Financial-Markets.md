# Lecture 6: Financial Markets — Money Markets, Bond/Debt Markets & FX Markets

## Key Concepts Covered

- **Money markets in India**: uncollateralized call/notice/term money; repo, CROMS, TREPS (successor to CBLO); CPs, CDs, T-bills, ABCPs (not permitted in India); Money Market Mutual Funds. 
  - **Call/Notice/Term Money**: Unsecured short-term lending (0-14 days for call/notice; 14 days to 1 year for term) between banks without collateral. Call money is overnight; notice money is 2-14 days; term money exceeds 14 days. Only SCBs (Scheduled Commercial Banks), RRBs (Regional Rural Banks), SFBs (Small Finance Banks), co-operative banks, and Primary Dealers can participate.
  - **CROMS** (Clearcorp Repo Order Matching System): Trading platform for market repos (buy-sell of government securities with commitment to reverse). Two types: Basket Repos (borrow against any security in a pool) and Special Repos (against specific securities).
  - **TREPS** (Tripartite Repo): Repo where CCIL acts as intermediary/third party managing collateral, settlement, and custody. Replaced CBLO in November 2018.
  - **CBLO** (Collateralized Borrowing & Lending Obligation): Earlier money market instrument (2003-2018) backed by securities; replaced by TREPS.
  - **CP** (Commercial Paper): Unsecured short-term debt (7 days to 1 year) issued by non-financial corporates, NBFCs to finance working capital. Issued at discount; minimum rating P2 required.
  - **CD** (Certificate of Deposit): Unsecured money market instrument issued by banks as receipt for fixed deposits (up to 1 year maturity).
  - **T-bills** (Treasury Bills): Government of India's short-term promissory notes (91/182/364 days), issued at discount (zero-coupon), redeemable at par. **Different from G-secs**: T-bills mature in ≤1 year; G-secs mature >1 year. T-bills have single cash flow; G-secs have coupon payments + principal.
  - **ABCPs** (Asset Backed Commercial Papers): Short-term secured papers backed by trade receivables or other assets. **NOT PERMITTED in India** — regulatory conservatism; ABCP markets froze during 2008 crisis when asset valuations became opaque.
  - **Money Market Mutual Funds** (MMMFs): Open-ended funds investing in T-bills, CPs, CDs (low-risk, short-term instruments) to maintain stable NAV. Offer better returns than savings accounts with minimal volatility. Started U.S. (1972) as Regulation Q workaround; came to India (1992). Not bank deposits (not insured), so carry default risk on underlying instruments (rare but possible).

Policy repo rate anchors money market rates within the SDF–MSF interest rate corridor. 

SDF Standing Deposit Facility, interest at which banks can park money with the RBI, this sets the floor, and banks would lend to each other only above this rate, else they would park with RBI to earn better.
Money markets act as an early-warning "plumbing" indicator of systemic stress (rate spikes, widening spreads, flight to safety, standing-facility usage).
- **Bond/debt market basics**: par value, coupon, YTM, price-yield inverse relationship, duration, convexity, coupon structures (fixed, floating, step-up, credit-linked, PIK, zero-coupon, perpetual), credit ratings (PD/LGD, national vs. global rating scales), green/blue/yellow/transition bonds.
  - **PIK** (Payment-in-Kind): Coupon structure where issuer pays interest in additional bonds instead of cash. Used when issuer is cash-constrained; signals financial weakness (if they could pay cash, they would).
  - **PD & LGD**: Two components of credit risk. **PD** (Probability of Default) = likelihood borrower won't repay. **LGD** (Loss Given Default) = loss if default occurs (total owed minus amount recovered from collateral/bankruptcy). CRAs assess PD (conventional ratings); LGD requires collateral analysis.
  - **Green Bonds**: Finance renewable energy, clean transport, sustainable water, climate adaptation, energy efficiency, waste management, forestry, biodiversity. **Blue Bonds**: Water/marine sector. **Yellow Bonds**: Solar energy specifically. **Transition Bonds**: Finance companies' shift from carbon-intensive to sustainable (e.g., coal company transitioning to renewables). All are "labeled bonds" — same credit quality as regular bonds, but issuer commits proceeds to green/social projects tracked via regulatory disclosures.

- **Corporate financing theory**: 
  - **Modigliani-Miller (MM) I**: In perfect markets (no taxes, no bankruptcy costs, symmetric information), firm value depends only on its assets, not capital structure (debt vs. equity mix). Unrealistic assumption; real markets have taxes, bankruptcy costs, information gaps.
  - **Modigliani-Miller II**: As debt increases, cost of equity rises (equity becomes riskier); however, tax shields on debt interest may make cost of equity less sensitive to leverage.
  - **Pecking Order Theory** (Myers-Majluf, 1984): Firms prefer: (1) Internal financing (retained earnings) — no scrutiny, no cost; (2) External debt — less scrutiny than equity, tax-deductible interest; (3) External equity — worst option because market interprets it as negative signal ("management knows bad news coming"). Explains why equity issuance often triggers stock decline.
  - **Financing Constraints** (Fazzari, Hubbard & Petersen, 1988): Capital markets aren't perfect; constrained firms (startups, poor credit history) can't borrow cheaply. When external shocks hit (sudden profit loss), constrained firms cut investment in profitable projects because they can't refinance — macroeconomic impact of financing crunches during recessions.
  - **Indian corporates' shift**: From heavy reliance on external equity (mid-1990s) to now matching pecking order theory — over 50% internal financing (reserves + depreciation), with smaller shares of external equity and bank borrowing. Leverage (debt-to-equity) at multi-year low (35.9% in 2023-24).

- **G-sec market**: institutional structure, auction mechanics, Primary Dealer system, fiscal deficit/public debt trends, debt sustainability, ownership pattern shifts, FPI routes, G-sec risks, RBI's failed soft yield-curve-control attempt during COVID.
  - **Debt Manager**: RBI's role managing government borrowing to finance fiscal deficit at lowest cost while mitigating risks (e.g., staggering maturity dates so government doesn't face sudden repayment peaks).
  - **IDMD** (Internal Debt Management Department): RBI department managing domestic debt of central government and 28 state governments. Plans borrowing calendar, conducts auctions, manages debt sustainability (Sections 20-21, RBI Act 1934).
  - **PDO** (Public Debt Office): RBI's registry/depository for G-secs. Issues new securities, pays coupons, repays principal at maturity. Acts as "back office" for G-sec operations.
  - **CCIL** (Clearing Corporation of India Limited): Central counterparty clearing house (established 2001, QCCP since 2014). Clears/settles money market, G-sec, FX trades. Operates CROMS (repo platform), TREPS (tri-party repo), F-TRAC (corporate bond repository). Reduces counterparty risk by standing between buyer and seller.
  - **FPI Routes for G-sec investment**:
    - **FAR** (Fully Accessible Route, April 2020): Non-resident investors, **NO investment cap**, can invest in 44 specified G-secs directly. Largest inflows (₹2,83,025 crore, Aug 2025) because no ceiling; strategic to attract foreign reserves and enable inclusion in global bond indices (achieved June 2024).
    - **VRR** (Voluntary Retention Route): Non-resident investors with commitment to hold for specified period; encourages long-term investment. Minimal inflows (₹1,005 crore) because investors prefer flexibility.
    - **MTF** (Medium-Term Framework) / General Route: Older FPI channel with investment caps. Still significant inflows (₹57,602 crore) due to institutional familiarity.
  - **RBI's Failed Soft Yield Curve Control (COVID-19)**: RBI tried keeping 10-year G-sec yield at/below 6% through price auctions and uniform-price auctions to reduce government borrowing costs during pandemic. **Failed because**: (1) market rejected unrealistic yield targets; (2) large auction portions went unsubscribed (no buyers at RBI's target yield); (3) RBI couldn't overpower market forces — had to abandon YCC and let yields rise to market-clearing levels. **Lesson**: Central banks control overnight rates via policy rate, but controlling long-term bond yields is near-impossible without sacrificing other objectives or unlimited bond purchases.
  - **G-sec risks**: Market risk (interest rate movements cause price losses), reinvestment risk (coupon/principal proceeds reinvested at lower rates if rates fall), liquidity risk (some securities become illiquid as they shorten in tenor or lack frequent reissuance).

- **FX markets**: OTC structure and turnover, FX swaps/spot dominance, USD dominance, Global FX Code, BIS's role, algo/HFT trading, carry trades, cross-currency basis trades, de-dollarization, swap lines and RFAs, Triffin Dilemma, and Exorbitant Privilege.
  - **OTC (Over-the-Counter)**: Direct trading between two parties without centralized exchange. $7.5tn/day (April 2022) — larger than all stock exchanges. FX swaps (51% turnover) and spot trades (28% turnover) dominate.
  - **FX Swaps**: Simultaneous buy-sell of two currencies with different delivery dates (e.g., exchange $100 for ₹8,300 today; reverse in 3 months). Manages currency risk without holding foreign currency long-term.
  - **Spot Trades**: Immediate currency exchange (settlement ~2 days). 28% of turnover.
  - **USD Dominance**: 88-89% of FX trades involve USD (one side of transaction). Driven by largest economy, liquidity, global reserve currency status, trade invoicing. Rising to 89.2% (2025) from 88% (2019).
  - **CNY/RMB Rising Share**: 4.3% (2019) → 7.0% (2022) → 8.5% (2025). Enabled by SDR inclusion (2016) and growing China trade role.
  - **INR Nascent Share**: 1.0% (2013) → 1.6% (2022) → 1.9% (2025). RBI's SAARC Swap Framework and bilateral swaps (e.g., India-Japan ¥75bn) boosting INR internationalization.
  - **Global FX Code** (May 2017, updated Dec 2024): Voluntary global principles for FX market integrity, transparency, ethics, governance, risk management, execution, confirmations, settlement. Created by central banks + private sector from 20 jurisdictions. Firms post commitment publicly on register. **Key**: voluntary, not regulatory; promotes level playing field.
  - **BIS** (Bank for International Settlements): Central bank cooperative institution (63 central banks = 95% world GDP). Hosts key committees: **BCBS** (banking capital standards), **CGFS** (financial markets monitoring), **Markets Committee** (market conditions, FX surveys), **CPMI** (payment/settlement safety), **GFXC** (Global FX Code), hosts **FSB** (Financial Stability Board). **BIS Rio Project**: Real-time FX market monitoring tool (cloud-based, AI-driven) launched April 2023 to detect dislocations and spillovers.
  - **Algo/HFT Trading**: ~70% of spot FX trades electronic. ~70% of EBS (Electronic Broking Services, CME platform) orders from algorithms. Concern: latency arbitrage. Mitigation: platforms introduced randomization (20-80ms pauses) to eliminate speed advantage.
  - **Carry Trade**: Borrow in low-interest currency (JPY, EUR, CHF), invest in high-yield currency (GBP, AUD, NZD, or exotic: BRL, INR, TRY, ZAR). Profit = yield difference. Often leveraged (margin borrowing amplifies gains/losses). **2024 Unwind**: JPY strengthened; yen-carry trades liquidated; leveraged positions amplified losses; Aug 5 peak: TOPIX lost 12% in one day, VIX spiked to COVID levels. Estimated ¥40tn ($250bn) carry trade size.
  - **Cross-Currency Basis Trade**: Arbitrage supply/demand mismatches via cross-currency swaps. Borrow one currency, lend another simultaneously. "Basis" = extra spread compensating for funding cost differences, liquidity gaps, credit risk. Widens during dollar shortages (high demand for USD) or capital flow imbalances.
  - **De-dollarization of Reserves**: USD share declined from ~70% (end-1999, peak 72% in 2001) to 56.8% (end-2025). **BUT slow-burn** (25 years). Euro: 33.4% (2013) → 28.9% (2025). CNY: 2.2% → 8.5%. Paradox: Most **trade still dollar-invoiced** — Americas 96%, Asia-Pacific 74%, Rest of World 79% (exception: Europe 66% euro). **Dominant Currency Paradigm** (Gita Gopinath concept): EM currency depreciation doesn't boost exports when trade is dollar-denominated; instead, imports become more expensive, causing inflation.
  - **Swap Lines (Bilateral)**: Currency exchange agreements between two central banks for emergency funding. Example: India-Japan ¥75bn swap; RBI's SAARC Swap (₹25,000cr INR + $2bn USD/EUR windows). Provide liquidity when dollar funding dries up.
  - **Regional Financing Arrangements (RFAs)**: Multilateral reserve pools (CMIM-ASEAN+, BRICS CRA, FLAR-Latin America, ESM-Eurozone). Strengthen **Global Financial Safety Net (GFSN)** — layered protection via IMF, bilateral swaps, regional arrangements.
  - **Triffin Dilemma / Paradox** (1960s, economist Robert Triffin): Conflict for reserve-currency countries. World wants to hold currency as safe reserves → country must run trade deficit to supply currency → deficits weaken currency → less attractive as reserve. Current: $9tn US Treasuries held by foreigners (32% of US debt) — unsustainable long-term if deficits persist.
  - **Exorbitant Privilege** (term by French finance minister Valéry Giscard d'Estaing, 1960s): US advantage from dollar dominance. High demand for dollars as reserves → demand for US debt high → US borrowing costs low (low Treasury rates). US effectively borrows cheaply; other countries pay higher rates. **Cost**: Must accept persistent trade deficits (export less, import more).

## Why This Matters (Accessibility)

This is predominantly **technical/institutional content** — heavy on market mechanics, instrument taxonomies, pricing formulas (YTM, duration, convexity) and India-specific regulatory plumbing (SEBI/RBI mandates, CCIL, CROMS). A student with only basic macro background (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, forex reserves) would find the bond-pricing and market-microstructure sections a real stretch, since they assume prior finance coursework. However, the FX/reserve-currency material (de-dollarization, Triffin Dilemma, swap lines, carry trades) connects naturally to macro/forex-reserve intuition and is more accessible narrative content.
