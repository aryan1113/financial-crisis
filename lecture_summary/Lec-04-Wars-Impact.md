# Lecture 4: Impacts of Wars on Economy & Financial Markets

## Key Concepts

- **Historical sweep**: origins and costs of WW-I and WW-II, the Cold War (USSR dissolution, Gorbachev's *perestroika*/*glasnost*), and how geopolitics has fed into current sovereign credit downgrades (US: Aaa→Aa1 by Moody's in 2025) and tariff escalation.
- **Rising conflict map (2025-26)**: Middle East wars (Gaza/Hamas-Israel, Lebanon/Hezbollah, Iran-US 12-day war and Strait of Hormuz closure/reopening), Russia-Ukraine War (~4 years, territorial gains largely stalled), and shifting alliances (Sunni "Diamond Alliance" vs. Israel-backed "Hexagon Alliance").
- **Cost of war/military economics**: defense spending as % of GDP ("guns vs. butter" tradeoff), the *Broken Window Fallacy* (war can boost measured GDP via destruction-driven activity despite being welfare-negative), missile/weapons cost breakdowns (Javelin, BrahMos), and global military spending rankings (US, China, Russia, Germany, India top five; Ukraine highest military burden as % of GDP).
- **Nuclear deterrence**: comparative warhead counts (Russia, US, India, Pakistan, China), no-first-use doctrines, and Pakistan's ambiguous "red lines" framework.
- **Macro-financial transmission channels**: commodity price shocks (global index +38.4% in H1-2022 post Ukraine invasion; oil +65% in early 2026 post Hormuz closure), inflation spikes in Russia/Ukraine and heterogeneous inflation effects across MENA economies, current account and fiscal balance divergence, and debt sustainability risk (MENA "perfect storm" - Bahrain, Egypt, Jordan, Tunisia, Lebanon).
- **India-specific spillovers**: GDP growth resilience amid the Iran war, fiscal slippage risk (Rs 3.7 tn) offset by RBI surplus transfer and reduced Contingency Risk Buffer, CAD/GDP range (1.2-2.5% of GDP FY27), and INR/bond yield movements tied to oil prices.
- **Global monetary policy divergence**: AE central banks split (ECB/BoJ/RBA hiking vs. Fed/BoE on hold per June 2026 FOMC statement), EM central banks caught between energy-driven inflation and growth risk, and open discussion questions on pre-emptive tightening vs. tolerating stagflationary shocks, and whether QE would recur as in GFC/pandemic.
- **Trade/dollar order shifts**: China's dominance in global merchandise trade, declining dollar share of global reserves (~70% in 1999 to ~56.8% in 2025), renminbi's SDR basket inclusion.

## Why This Matters

This lecture is mostly **conceptual/narrative** (geopolitical history, alliance structures, qualitative cost accounting) but pivots into **quantitative macro-financial data** in its second half (inflation rates, CPI figures, fiscal deficit numbers, CAD/GDP ranges, central bank policy rates). For someone with only basic macroeconomics background (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, forex reserves), the historical/geopolitical sections are **fully accessible**, and the macro-transmission sections (commodity shock → inflation → monetary policy response → exchange rate) map directly onto standard macro intuition, making this lecture **broadly accessible with only mild stretch** in the India fiscal-detail and central bank balance-sheet sections.

## Relevance to the Crisis Simulation

**High.** The lecture directly supplies the causal chain (war/geopolitical shock → commodity/oil price spike → inflation and currency pressure → fiscal slippage and monetary policy dilemma) that RBI Governor/Deputy Governors, Finance Minister/Secretary, and Fed Chair roles would need to reason through, plus discussion questions (pre-emptive tightening vs. stagflation tolerance, EM rate defense) that mirror exactly the decisions a role-play would require.
