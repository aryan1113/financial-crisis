# Lecture 1: Introduction to Financial Crises: definitions, typology, and historical sweep

## Key Concepts

- **Defining terms**: "Financial" relates to finance broadly; "crisis" originated as a medical term (turning point of disease) and evolved to mean a decisive stage of stress requiring urgent action.
- **Three types of financial crises**: Sovereign debt crises (unsustainable public debt), banking crises (panics/bank runs), and currency crises (exchange rate misalignment, capital flow volatility, reserve loss).
- **Kindleberger-Minsky model**: Minsky's credit cycle — lending progresses through hedge, speculative, and Ponzi stages, generating five bubble phases: displacement, boom, euphoria, profit-taking, panic. Includes the Bagehot Rule (central banks should lend freely to solvent institutions against good collateral, at a penal rate) as the basis of the Lender of Last Resort function.
- **"This time is different" syndrome** (Reinhart & Rogoff): policymakers repeatedly ignore bubble warning signs, believing current conditions are structurally different from history.
- **Anna Karenina Principle**: healthy economies share common traits (sustainable growth, low/stable inflation, sustainable fiscal deficit and current account, adequate bank buffers, healthy corporate balance sheets), while crises arise idiosyncratically.
- **Historical case survey** (brief, scene-setting only): Tulip Mania, Mississippi Bubble, South Sea Bubble, the Great Depression, Bretton Woods (1944), Latin American Debt Crisis (1980s), ERM/Black Wednesday (1992), Mexican Peso "Tequila" Crisis (1994-95), Asian Financial Crisis (1997-98), Taper Tantrum (2013), Global Financial Crisis (2007-09), COVID-19 (2020-21).
- **Course logistics**: instructor background (ex-RBI Executive Director/MPC member), grading, reading approach, and structure of the term's role-play simulation.

## Why This Matters

This is primarily **conceptual/narrative**, not quantitative — a framing and history lecture with almost no formal models, equations, or technical derivations (a few historical statistics tables are illustrative, not analytical). Someone with only basic macroeconomics (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, forex reserves) should find this fully accessible; the vocabulary (sovereign debt, banking panics, currency pegs, capital flight) builds directly on those fundamentals without requiring deeper technical background.
