# Lecture 14: Taper Tantrum 2013

## Key Concepts

- **What happened**: In May 2013, Fed Chair Ben Bernanke hinted at tapering the Fed's QE bond-buying programme; US bond yields surged, triggering capital outflows from emerging markets (EMs) via a bond sell-off → currency depreciation → equity sell-off → derivatives-amplified "mayhem" chain. Not a full crisis, but a near-miss, contained mainly by later central bank communication.
- **The "Fragile Five"**: Brazil, India, Indonesia, South Africa, Turkey — the EMs hit hardest, generally those with weak macro fundamentals (high CAD, high inflation, low reserves, weak growth).
- **FOMC timeline (Jan-Dec 2013)**: internal Fed dissents (Esther George's 7 dissents pushing for earlier taper vs. Bernanke's caution), Bernanke's 22 May testimony sparking the tantrum, the 19 June press conference detailing a $85bn→$65bn/month taper path and the "Evans Rule" (2% inflation / 6.5% unemployment thresholds for rate hikes), repeated delays through mid-2013 amid market turmoil, the Oct debt-ceiling scare, and the actual taper announcement in Dec 2013 (start Jan 2014), which markets absorbed calmly since it was already priced in.
- **Quantitative tools mentioned**: Z-scores (standardizing asset-price moves to compare volatility across countries) and an Augmented GARCH/M-GARCH/BEKK model framework with a taper-talk dummy variable, used to statistically confirm significant EM volatility spillovers in stock, bond, and FX markets during May-Aug 2013 vs. before/after.
- **India-specific impact**: growth slowdown (5.2-5.5%), CPI inflation peaking at 11.2% (Nov 2013), twin deficit stress (fiscal deficit ~4.5% of GDP, CAD spiking to 4.9% of GDP in Q1 before correcting to 1.7% for the year), INR depreciating 17.9% (May-Sept 2013), ~US$13.4bn FII outflows, and Raghuram Rajan's appointment as RBI Governor (Sept 2013) as a market-confidence turning point.
- **RBI policy response**: liquidity tightening (OMO sales, LAF caps), a 300bps "stealth tightening" via MSF rate hikes, capital flow management measures (gold import duty hikes, 80:20 scheme), and the FCNR(B) swap window / overseas borrowing concessions that pulled in US$34.3bn to rebuild FX reserve buffers.
- **Lessons drawn**: central bank communication discipline is critical under multiple-equilibria market sentiment; systemically important central banks must weigh cross-border spillovers; markets initially herd indiscriminately but later differentiate by fundamentals; early/decisive EM action helps; strong reserves and macro fundamentals are the best defense (more than reliance on global financial safety nets like the IMF).

## Why This Matters

This lecture blends **narrative/historical content** (Fed decision timeline, RBI response, Rajan's appointment) with a **technical layer** (Z-scores and GARCH-family volatility models). Someone with only basic macro background (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, reserves) will find the narrative and India-impact sections fully accessible, but the GARCH/M-GARCH/BEKK econometrics section is a genuine stretch — it requires some background in time-series volatility modeling to interpret meaningfully, even though the qualitative takeaway (EM spillovers were statistically significant) can be understood without the math.
