# Lecture 14: Taper Tantrum 2013
## How a Fed Announcement Shook Emerging Markets

---

## 1. The Shock: Fed Signals Taper

### What Happened
In May 2013, Federal Reserve Chair Ben Bernanke hinted that the Fed would soon taper (reduce) its Quantitative Easing (QE) bond-buying program.

### The Chain Reaction
- US bond yields surged (Treasury returns suddenly attractive)
- Investors pulled money from emerging markets (EM) to get higher US returns
- **Capital outflow**: Bond sell-off → currency depreciation → equity sell-off → credit tightening
- **Result**: Near-crisis in vulnerable EMs (but not a full crisis—contained later by better communication)

---

## 2. The Victims: The "Fragile Five"

### Which Countries Were Hit Hardest
| Country | Main Vulnerabilities |
|---------|----------------------|
| **Brazil** | High CAD, high inflation, low reserves |
| **India** | Twin deficit, weak reserves, high inflation |
| **Indonesia** | High external debt, slow growth |
| **South Africa** | Current account deficit, low reserves |
| **Turkey** | Current account deficit, inflation |

### The Common Thread
All five had **weak macro fundamentals** going into 2013—making them vulnerable to sudden capital flight.

---

## 3. Inside the Federal Reserve: The FOMC Timeline

### January–May 2013: Growing Dissent
- **Esther George** (Fed Governor) pushed hard for earlier taper (7 public dissents)
- **Ben Bernanke** (Fed Chair) was more cautious about timing

### May 22, 2013: The Tantrum Begins
- Bernanke's Congressional testimony hinted at taper
- Markets panicked: emerging markets started selling off immediately

### June 19, 2013: The Taper Path
- FOMC press conference outlined the taper:
  - From **$85 billion/month → $65 billion/month**
  - Linked to the **"Evans Rule"**: Rate hikes when inflation hits 2% OR unemployment falls to 6.5%
- Supposed to be calming but wasn't

### July–December 2013: Repeated Delays
- Markets continued to sell EM assets
- October 2013: US debt-ceiling crisis created additional shock
- Fed repeatedly delayed actual taper announcement

### December 2013: The Actual Taper Begins
- Fed announces taper starting January 2014
- **Calm market absorption**: By then, markets had priced it in
- **Lesson**: Uncertainty and surprise are worse than bad news; clarity helps even if news is unwelcome

---

## 4. India's Specific Hit (May–September 2013)

### The Impact on Growth & Inflation
- **Growth slowed** to 5.2–5.5% (below potential)
- **Inflation spiked** to 11.2% (November 2013 peak)
- Double trouble: slow growth + high inflation (stagflation)

### Twin Deficit Stress
| Deficit | Peak Impact |
|---------|-------------|
| **Fiscal deficit** | ~4.5% of GDP |
| **Current account deficit (CAD)** | 4.9% of GDP (Q1 2013) then 1.7% (full year) |

### Currency Collapse
- **INR depreciation**: 17.9% (May–September 2013)
- Falls from ~55 to ~65 per USD
- Makes imports more expensive; exports cheaper but less immediate

### Capital Flight
- **FII (Foreign Institutional Investor) outflows**: ~$13.4 billion
- Foreign investors leaving India

---

## 5. The Turning Point: Raghuram Rajan as RBI Governor

### Market Confidence Moment
In **September 2013**, Raghuram Rajan was appointed RBI Governor.

**Market reaction**: Immediate stabilization

**Why**: Rajan had global credibility as an economist and reputation for decisive action. Markets believed he would handle the crisis competently.

**Lesson**: **Central bank credibility and leadership matter enormously during crises.**

---

## 6. RBI's Policy Response Toolkit

### 1. Monetary Tightening: Make Borrowing Expensive
The RBI raised rates to make it expensive to borrow, discouraging short-term speculation and defending the currency.

#### Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) Rate Hike
- **What it is**: The most expensive overnight borrowing rate for banks
- **RBI action**: Raised it **200 basis points to 10.25%**
- **Effect**: Made emergency bank borrowing very expensive; discouraged lending for speculation

#### LAF (Liquidity Adjustment Facility) Caps
- **What it is**: A mechanism where banks borrow from RBI at lower rates
- **RBI action**: Capped access (1.0% of NDTL system-wide, later 0.5% per bank)
- **Effect**: Forced banks to reduce easy borrowing; tightened money supply

### 2. Supply-Side Measures: Remove Cash

#### OMO (Open Market Operations) Sales
- **What**: RBI sold government securities (₹120 billion)
- **Effect**: Removed cash from the banking system; reduced liquidity

### 3. Currency Defense: Attract Foreign Currency

#### FCNR(B) Swap Window
- **What**: Attracted deposits from overseas Indians (NRIs) in foreign currency
- **How**: RBI offered 3.5% per annum swap rate (vs. 7% regular hedging costs)
- **Result**: Cheaper way for Indians abroad to park money and for RBI to raise forex
- **Amount**: Raised ~$34.3 billion in FX reserves

### 4. Capital Flow Management: Reduce Imports

#### Gold Import Duty Hike
- **Goal**: Reduce gold imports (saves forex)
- **Rationale**: Gold is a non-essential import that drains forex

#### 80:20 Gold Scheme
- **Rule**: Gold importers had to export 20% of their imports
- **Effect**: Reduced net gold inflows; conserved forex

---

## 7. Market Stabilization: How It Worked

### The Sequence
1. **Rate hikes** → Makes rupee defense more credible; raises cost of betting against rupee
2. **Foreign currency raised** → Shows RBI has buffers to defend peg if needed
3. **Communication** → Rajan's credibility signals RBI has strategy
4. **Time** → Markets calm as Fed clarifies taper path (and delays)

### Result
By **October–November 2013**: Stabilization emerges. The rupee stops collapsing. FII outflows slow.

---

## 8. Key Lessons

### 1. Central Bank Communication is Critical
- Uncertainty is worse than bad news
- Fed's vague taper hints in May caused more damage than the December clarity
- Clear forward guidance helps markets adjust

### 2. Systemically Important Central Banks Must Consider Spillovers
- Fed Chair's words matter globally
- A taper designed for the US economy sideswipes emerging markets
- Global coordination and sequencing matter

### 3. Markets Herd, Then Differentiate
- **Initial phase**: Panic selling, indiscriminate capital flight from ALL EMs
- **Later phase**: Markets calm and reward countries with strong fundamentals and credible central banks

### 4. Early & Decisive Action Helps
- India's rate hikes and forex management showed resolve
- Markets responded to clear policy framework
- Rajan's appointment was a confidence signal

### 5. Strong Reserves & Macro Fundamentals are the Best Defense
- Countries with:
  - High forex reserves
  - Low inflation
  - Fiscal discipline
  - Strong growth
- ...weathered the tantrum better
- IMF/safety nets are backstop, not primary defense
