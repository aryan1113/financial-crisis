# Lecture 15: COVID-19 Pandemic Crisis — global and Indian macro-financial response, scarring, and lessons for resilience

## Key Concepts

- **Health-to-market transmission**: pandemic waves (first wave, Delta, Omicron) tanked asset prices, drove >20% EM currency drops, >US$100bn EME capital outflows (exceeding GFC), and commodity price collapses.
- **Growth & inflation whipsaw**: demand collapse initially pushed inflation down; supply-chain disruption + Ukraine war later pushed it up sharply (AE and EME inflation both overshot targets, then central banks tightened aggressively and largely tamed it).
- **RBI's crisis response**: advanced MPC meeting (March 2020), 75bps repo cut (4-2 majority) to 4.4%, reverse repo cut, liquidity measures (LTRO, TLTRO, CRR cuts, G-SAP, SLF) totaling ~₹17.22 lakh crore (8.7% of GDP).
- **Unconventional Monetary Policy Tools (UMPTs)**: QE/LSAPs, negative rates (ECB, BoJ), forward guidance, Operation Twist, yield curve control — used by Fed (~$2tn QE), ECB (PEPP €750bn), BoE, and EM variants (RBI's GSAP).
- **Modern Monetary Theory (MMT) vs. Fiscal Theory of Price Level (FTPL)** — critiqued as risky ("playing with fire"), can cause hyperinflation-style outcomes.
- **Hysteresis/"Scarring"**: pandemic caused persistent output losses via firm capital destruction and job losses; India's growth trend delayed recovery to loss-recoupment by 2034-35.
- **Structural shifts**: K-shaped recovery (inequality widened), digitalization acceleration (partly transient), reverse/donut migration patterns, Doughnut Economics framing.
- **Post-crisis debt overhang**: unsustainable public debt buildup globally; DSSI (Debt Service Suspension Initiative) as a relief mechanism.
- **Resilience-building themes**: rebuilding social contracts, unwinding UMPTs/QT, fiscal space creation, health system resilience, climate change as a future shock vector.

## Why This Matters

This lecture is largely **conceptual/narrative with embedded quantitative detail** (rates, amounts, GDP ratios, timelines) rather than a technical/analytical framework lecture. A student with only basic macro background (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, forex reserves) should find most of it **accessible** — the RBI response, QE/UMPT mechanics, and inflation dynamics build directly on those foundations. The stretch points are MMT/FTPL nuances, hysteresis/scarring channels, and the more sociological material (Doughnut Economics, migration patterns, social contracts) — these are more discursive and don't require quantitative modeling to follow.
