# Lecture 15: COVID-19 Pandemic Crisis
## Global and Indian Macro-Financial Response, Scarring, and Resilience Lessons

---

## 1. The Shock: Health Crisis → Financial Crisis

### How Pandemic Spread to Markets
- **Pandemic waves** → Asset price collapses
- **Capital flight from EMs**: >$100 billion (larger than the 2008 GFC outflows)
- **Currency collapses**: >20% EM currency depreciation
- **Commodity price crashes**: Oil, metals, agricultural goods

---

## 2. The Growth-Inflation Whipsaw

### Phase 1: Demand Collapse (March–May 2020)
- Economic shutdown → lower demand
- **Inflation fell temporarily** (less spending = lower prices)
- Deflation fears

### Phase 2: Supply Disruption (2021–2022)
- Supply chains broke (lockdowns, ports closed)
- Manufacturers couldn't get inputs
- Combined with **Ukraine war** shock
- **Inflation surged** (shortages + stimulus = too much money chasing too few goods)

### Central Banks Respond
- **Advanced Economies (AE)** and **Emerging Market Economies (EME)**: Both saw inflation overshoot targets
- **Response**: Aggressive rate hikes through 2022–2023
- **Result**: Largely tamed inflation by 2023, but with growth cost

---

## 3. RBI's Crisis Playbook (March 2020)

### The Decisions
**Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) meeting, March 2020:**
- **Repo rate cut**: 75 basis points, bringing repo to 4.4% (4-2 vote)
- **Reverse repo cut**: Also reduced (makes it cheaper for banks to lend rather than park funds with RBI)

### The Liquidity Tools Deployed
Total liquidity injection: **~₹17.22 lakh crore (8.7% of GDP)**

| Program | Amount | Purpose | Recovery |
|---------|--------|---------|----------|
| **LTRO** (Long-Term Refinancing Operation) | ₹2,00,000 cr | 3-year cheap loans to banks | 98.8% repaid |
| **TLTRO** (Targeted LTRO) | ₹1,00,000 cr | Cheap loans conditional on lending to SMEs | 33.1% repaid |
| **CRR cuts** (Cash Reserve Ratio) | ₹1,37,000 cr | Reduced reserves banks must hold | Later restored to 4.0% |
| **G-SAP** (Govt Securities Acquisition Programme) | ₹2,20,000 cr | RBI bond buying + regular OMOs | Ongoing |
| **SLF** (Standing Liquidity Facility) | ₹7,200 cr | Emergency overnight facility for dealers | As needed |

### The Strategy
1. Cut rates sharply to incentivize lending
2. Inject massive liquidity through multiple channels
3. Target specific sectors (SMEs via TLTRO)
4. Provide emergency backstops (SLF)

## 4. Global Unconventional Monetary Policy Tools

### What Are UMPTs?
**Unconventional Monetary Policy Tools (UMPTs)**: Non-standard tools used when traditional rate cuts reach zero.

### Examples

| Tool | Used By | Size | Purpose |
|------|---------|------|---------|
| **QE (Large-Scale Asset Purchases)** | Fed, ECB, BoE | Fed: ~$2 trillion | Buy bonds to inject money |
| **PEPP** (Pandemic Emergency Purchase Programme) | ECB | €750 billion | Bond buying during pandemic |
| **Negative Rates** | ECB, BoJ | -0.5% to -1.0% | Penalize hoarding; force lending |
| **Forward Guidance** | All major CBs | -- | Commit to future policy |
| **Yield Curve Control** | BoJ, RBA | Varies | Target specific bond yields |
| **Operation Twist** | Fed | $2 trillion | Buy long-term, sell short-term bonds |

### The Risk: Playing With Fire?
- **Debate**: MMT (Modern Monetary Theory) vs. FTPL (Fiscal Theory of Price Level)
- **Criticism**: Risky policies; can lead to hyperinflation if coordination fails
- **Reality**: Used extensively; some worked, some left legacies (high debt, inequality)

## 5. Hysteresis: The Permanent Scars

### Definition: Scarring from Large Shocks
**Hysteresis**: A shock's effects persist long after it ends. The economy doesn't simply bounce back—it settles at a lower level permanently.

### COVID Scarring in India
- **Firm destruction**: Small businesses collapsed; capital lost
- **Job losses**: Workers laid off; some never rehired
- **Skill depreciation**: Unemployed workers lose skills
- **Result**: India's growth trend delayed recovery to **2034–35**

### The Impact
Even though growth resumed in 2021–22, **permanent output loss** from pandemic destruction meant lower long-term growth path.

---

## 6. The K-Shaped Recovery: Unequal Bounce-Back

### What Is K-Shaped?
**Definition**: Some sectors recover quickly (one arm of K) while others stay depressed (other arm of K).

### Winners: Digital & E-commerce Sectors
- **ICT (Information & Communication Technology)**: Boomed during WFH
- **E-commerce**: Surged as shopping moved online
- **Digitally-enabled firms**: ~4% average sales decline during pandemic

### Losers: Contact-Intensive Services
- **Restaurants, travel, hospitality**: Stayed depressed long-term
- **Non-digital firms**: Much larger sales declines (sometimes >50%)

### Social Impact
- K-shaped recovery widened **inequality**
- Digital haves pulled ahead; analog firms fell behind

---

## 7. Structural Shifts from COVID

### 1. Digitalization Acceleration
**Forced adoption**: COVID pushed firms and workers to go digital overnight.

**Resilience**: Digitally-ready firms were ~4x less affected.

**Legacy**: Post-pandemic, hybrid work became normal; digital services expanded.

### 2. Migration Patterns Reversed
- **Pre-pandemic**: Urban migration, megacities booming
- **Post-pandemic**: Reverse migration to smaller cities/towns
- **Reason**: Remote work freed people to live where they wanted

### 3. Rethinking Economics: Doughnut Economics
**Holistic approach**: Sustainability beyond GDP—focus on wellbeing, ecological limits, social foundations.

## 8. Debt Overhang & Relief Mechanisms

### The Post-Crisis Debt Problem
**Global public debt** ballooned from fiscal stimulus:
- Governments borrowed heavily to fund relief
- Some countries hit unsustainable debt levels

### DSSI (Debt Service Suspension Initiative)
- **What**: Allows poorest countries to pause debt payments temporarily
- **Purpose**: Free up cash for health/social spending during crisis
- **Limitation**: Only temporary relief; debt still owed later

---

## 9. Building Resilience for the Next Crisis

### Unwinding Crisis Measures
- **QT (Quantitative Tightening)**: Central banks shrink balance sheets, drain liquidity
- **Fiscal adjustment**: Governments return to sustainable deficit levels
- **Risk**: Too fast unwinding triggers new crises (as happened 2022–23)

### Long-Term Resilience Building

#### 1. Rebuilding Social Contracts
- COVID exposed gaps in safety nets
- Need stronger unemployment insurance, healthcare, education systems
- Social stability requires fairness and opportunity

#### 2. Fiscal Space Creation
- Can't sustain high debt forever
- Need to:
  - Raise revenues (taxes)
  - Control spending growth
  - Improve tax compliance
- **Goal**: Reach sustainable debt-to-GDP ratios

#### 3. Health System Resilience
- Pre-pandemic: Many countries underinvested in healthcare
- **Lesson**: Pandemics happen; need robust health capacity
- Requires:
  - Hospital beds, ICU capacity
  - Emergency supplies (PPE, ventilators)
  - Training and staffing

#### 4. Climate Change as a Future Shock Vector
- Pandemics = wake-up call that major shocks happen
- **Climate change**: Potentially larger shock ahead
- **Resilience needs**: Green infrastructure, adaptation capacity, just transitions
