# Lecture 3: Revisiting Macroeconomic Aggregates & Financial Indicators (GDP, BoP, Monetary & Fiscal Statistics)

## Key Concepts

- **GDP & National Accounts**: GDP definition (final goods/services, within-country, flow measure); three approaches (production=income=expenditure, Y=C+I+G+X-M); NDP, GNP, factor cost vs market price, real vs nominal GDP, GDP deflator; what's excluded from GDP (intermediate goods, used goods, non-market/household work, illegal economy, transfers, foreign-owned profits); System of National Accounts (SNA) history and 2025 revision (digital economy, natural capital).
- **India growth snapshot**: Q1 FY26 real GDP growth (7.8%), consumption and services/GVA-led growth trends.
- **Balance of Payments (BoP)**: definition, current/capital/financial account structure (BPM5 vs BPM6 vs new BPM7); components of current account (goods, services, primary income, secondary income); savings-investment identity (S-I = CAB); why CAD isn't inherently "bad" for EMEs.
- **India's BoP history as crisis case studies**: 1964-66 BoP crisis (war/drought-driven FX depletion, TTK's resistance to devaluation, 1966 57% rupee devaluation under Indira Gandhi); 1991 BoP crisis (twin deficits, Gulf War oil shock, political instability, gold pledged with Bank of England, Narasimha Rao-era devaluation).
- **FDI vs FPI**, capital flows, Net International Investment Position (NIIP) — India's NIIP is negative (net debtor).
- **External debt sustainability**: short-term vs long-term debt, debt/reserves ratios, India's external debt data (end-June 2024).
- **Why hold FX reserves**: buffer against sudden stops, FX intervention, confidence signaling; costs of reserves/sterilization.
- **Trade theory**: Ricardian comparative advantage, Heckscher-Ohlin, strategic trade theory; Bhagwati's "trade in widgets vs. trade in dollars" (Asian Financial Crisis 1997-98 context); Marshall-Lerner condition and J-curve for devaluation efficacy.
- **Monetary statistics**: reserve/base money, CRR, money supply components, RBI's internal reserves/Economic Capital Framework (CGRA, IRA, contingency fund), RBI surplus transfers to government.
- **Core inflation**: exclusion-based vs trimmed-mean measures.
- **Fiscal statistics**: gross/net fiscal deficit, revenue deficit, primary deficit; Centre and States' fiscal trends; Domar condition (r < g) for debt sustainability; IMF/World Bank Debt Sustainability Framework; Paris Club/London Club debt restructuring mechanisms.

## Why This Matters (Accessibility)

This is largely **technical/definitional** content — precise accounting identities (GDP components, BoP sub-accounts, deficit types) mixed with **narrative case studies** (1966 and 1991 crises). For someone with only basic macro background (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, FX reserves), the historical narratives are very accessible, but the BoP sub-account taxonomy (BPM6 categories, NIIP mechanics, Domar condition) and fiscal deficit definitions are a moderate stretch — they require careful reading but no advanced math.

## Relevance to the Crisis Simulation: **High**

This lecture is essentially the vocabulary and institutional memory the simulation runs on — the 1966 and 1991 BoP crisis narratives directly mirror the roles being simulated (RBI Governor/Deputies, Finance Minister, IMF officials), and concepts like FX reserves adequacy, external debt sustainability, devaluation trade-offs, and fiscal deficit reporting are exactly what those roles would need to argue from in-character.
