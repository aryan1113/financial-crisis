# Lecture 12: The Euro Area (Eurozone) Sovereign Debt Crisis, 2009-12

## Key Concepts

- **Origins and spread**: Began in Greece in 2009, spread to Cyprus and the PIGS (Portugal, Italy, Greece, Spain), later PIIGS after Ireland joined — countries unable to refinance public debt, facing sovereign default risk.
- **The "diabolic/doom loop"**: A self-reinforcing link between sovereign debt and bank debt — bank bailouts pushed public debt to unsustainable levels, which in turn weakened the banks holding that debt.
- **BOP dimension**: The crisis manifested as a balance-of-payments crisis via a sudden stop of capital inflows to Southern periphery economies.
- **Troika bailouts**: Three Greek bailout packages (2010: ~€110bn; 2012: ~€130bn with private bondholder haircuts; 2015: up to €86bn) negotiated by the "Troika" — European Commission, ECB, and IMF. Ireland, Portugal, Spain, and Cyprus also received rescue packages (2010-13).
- **Firewall institutions**: European Financial Stability Facility (EFSF, 2010, temporary) evolved into the European Stability Mechanism (ESM, 2012, permanent) — a €500bn-capacity Regional Financing Arrangement (RFA) complementing the IMF within the Global Financial Safety Net (GFSN).
- **ECB liquidity tools**: 3-year LTROs (2011-12) to ease bank funding stress; TLTROs (from 2014, three rounds) linking cheap ECB borrowing to bank lending to firms/households, extended through COVID-19.
- **Draghi's "whatever it takes" (July 2012)**: A forward-guidance speech that sharply compressed peripheral bond spreads (500bps to 100-200bps), reportedly saving over €200bn in funding costs and marking the crisis turning point.
- **OMT (Outright Monetary Transactions)**: A conditional ECB bond-buying framework announced Sept 2012 (never actually used) requiring a country to be under an EFSF/ESM program with MOU conditionality and to have regained market access.
- **Political fallout**: Power shifts in 10 of 19 eurozone countries; austerity backlash, especially in Greece.
- **Lessons**: Sovereign debt risk is not only an EMDE problem; monetary union needs fiscal union and banking union; forward guidance is powerful; clear legal crisis-resolution frameworks and RFAs matter.

## Why This Matters

This is largely **conceptual/narrative** content — institutional history, policy sequencing, and crisis mechanics — rather than quantitative modeling. There are some numbers (bailout sizes, spread levels, facility capacities) but no formulas or derivations. A student with only basic macro background (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, forex reserves) should find this **accessible**, since the core ideas (debt sustainability, capital flow sudden-stops, lender-of-last-resort action, conditionality) map onto familiar BOP/monetary concepts, though EU-specific institutional alphabet soup (EFSF/ESM/OMT/LTRO/TLTRO/Troika) requires some memorization.

## Relevance to the Role-Play Simulation

**Medium.** The Euro crisis offers strong analogies for IMF-related roles (Troika-style conditionality, bailout negotiation, MOU design) and for Fed/central-bank roles (forward guidance, backstop facilities), but it is a currency-union crisis without an RBI-equivalent monetary-sovereignty dimension, so its direct transferability to an India-centric BOP/reserves crisis simulation is only partial.
