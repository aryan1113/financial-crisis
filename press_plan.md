1. Assign angles, not scripts — one person hits reserves/currency numbers, one hits accountability ("did you see this coming, why didn't you act sooner"), one hits the political fallout ("will there be resignations"), one hits the ordinary-citizen impact ("what does this mean for a saver/depositor tomorrow"). This lets 3-4 press members ask different things instead of overlapping, which reads as a real newsroom dividing a story.
2. Nominate someone to actually listen and cross-question — the single best move is picking one person whose entire job is to catch a contradiction or evasion in the central bank's opening statement and immediately follow up on it live ("You just said reserves are adequate, but you also said intervention continues daily — which is it?"). This is what made the opposition's attack land: it looked like genuine listening, not a pre-written monologue.
3. Pre-load crisis-agnostic follow-up templates they can adapt in seconds once the real topic is known — e.g.:
  - "You've announced [action]. Why not [more aggressive alternative]? Is that being held in reserve because things are worse than stated?"
  - "Has the government approached the IMF? If not yet, at what point does inaction become the story?"
  - "Who takes the blame if this doesn't work — was this foreseeable?"
4. Have one person target the Finance Ministry/opposition dynamic specifically, since that's the exact interaction Saggar singled out as realistic — press asking a question that forces daylight between the central bank's and the government's position ("The Finance Minister said X, but you just said Y — which of you is right?").

├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Sudden shock, need cash fast, no time for negotiation │ RFI (Rapid Financing Instrument) │ Emergency, no conditionality reviews, outright disbursement        │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Ongoing BoP problem, standard case                    │ SBA (Stand-By Arrangement)       │ The "default" IMF loan, up to 3 yrs, ex-post conditions            │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Deep structural problem (not just a cash crunch)      │ EFF (Extended Fund Facility)     │ Longer term (up to 4 yrs), tied to structural reforms              │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Country hasn't asked for money yet, wants insurance   │ FCL (Flexible Credit Line)       │ "We're fine, but just in case" — for strong-fundamentals countries │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Poor/low-income country version of the above          │ ECF/SCF/RCF (PRGT-funded)        │ Same logic as EFF/SBA/RFI but concessional, for poorer countries   │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Media angle on this: which facility is chosen is itself a story. RFI/RCF = "things are bad and urgent, no time for a program." SBA/EFF = "this is a negotiated, conditional loan — watch for austerity backlash." FCL = "we're not in crisis, this is just a signal" — good gotcha question to the Finance Minister if they're downplaying severity while quietly negotiating one.

4. Who's in the room — and the one killer question for each

- RBI Governor — overall face of the crisis response. Ask: "Is the central bank's independence being compromised by government pressure to act faster/slower?"
- RBI DG (Monetary Policy) — rate decisions, inflation targeting. Ask: "Are you raising rates to defend the currency at the cost of growth?"
- RBI DG (Financial Markets) — FX intervention, reserves. Ask: "How many billions in reserves have you burned defending the rupee this week, and what's the red line before you stop?"
- RBI DG (Regulation & Supervision) — bank health, NPAs. Ask: "Which banks are under your watch right now, and is a bailout being prepared?"
- Finance Minister / Finance Secretary — fiscal response, political optics, IMF negotiation. Ask: "Is the government approaching the IMF, and if so, why the delay?" (Delay in approaching IMF is explicitly an evaluation point per Lec-1.)
- IMF Mission Chief / Deputy Mission Chief — conditionality terms. Ask: "What conditionalities are non-negotiable, and will there be austerity measures that hurt ordinary citizens?"
- IMF ED India — India's seat at the IMF board, quota/voting angle. Ask: "Is India's IMF voice big enough to shape these terms, or are you just a rubber stamp?"
- Fed Chair — external trigger (US rate hikes cause EM outflows historically — Volcker shock, Taper Tantrum). Ask: "Is US monetary tightening the real cause of this crisis, and do you bear any responsibility for the spillover?"
