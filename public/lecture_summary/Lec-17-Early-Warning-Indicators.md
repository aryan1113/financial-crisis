# Lecture 17: Early Warning Indicators of Financial Crises (Mridul Saggar, IIM Kozhikode)

## Key Concepts
- Indicators are classified as leading, coincident, lagging, or unrelated to crisis; crisis episodes are scored as binary (1 = crisis, 0 = otherwise) for empirical/statistical testing.
- **Banking crisis leading indicators:**
  - Private credit boom (domestic credit growing ~2%+ above long-term trend)
  - Rising loan-to-deposit ratio (banks lending more than deposits taken in)
  - Heavy reliance on short-term wholesale funding for long-term loans
  - BIS credit-to-GDP gap (credit-to-GDP ratio minus its long-run trend, trend estimated via one-sided Hodrick-Prescott filter) — a key BIS early-warning metric
  - ECB research: domestic private credit, FDI inflow, world inflation, money market interest rates, world GDP, trade balance, trade as % of GDP, real effective exchange rate, general government balance
  - Saggar's own view: stress in the money market (entities trading at off-market rates) is the single best leading indicator of a banking crisis
- **Currency crisis leading indicators** (Kaminsky, Lizondo & Reinhart): international reserves, real exchange rate, credit growth, inflation, GDP growth/level/output gap, money supply, M2/international reserves ratio, short-term debt, FDI, domestic-foreign interest rate differential, public debt, employment/unemployment, change in stock prices.
- Slides also reference literature on banking crises, sovereign debt crises, currency crises, and financial crises generally (titles only, content not captured in OCR).

## Why This Matters
The lecture is a mix of conceptual framing (leading/coincident/lagging classification) and quantitative/technical content (credit-to-GDP gap, HP filter, empirical indicator lists from BIS/ECB/Kaminsky-Lizondo-Reinhart). Someone with only basic macro background (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, forex reserves) will recognize most named variables, but the credit-to-GDP gap and HP filter methodology are a moderate stretch — they'd need a plain-language explanation ("HP filter smooths out a long-run trend from noisy data") rather than the underlying math.
