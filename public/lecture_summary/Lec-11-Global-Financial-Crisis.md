# Lecture 11: The Global Financial Crisis (GFC), 2007-09 — Origins, Unfolding, and Policy Response

## Key Concepts

- **Definition & scale**: GFC (mid-2007 to mid-2009), originating as the US sub-prime crisis, spread globally and triggered the Great Recession — the worst downturn since 1929-39.
- **Regulatory seeds**: 1992 Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac affordable-housing mandates; 1995 Community Reinvestment Act changes fueling sub-prime lending; 1999 Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act repealing Glass-Steagall (ending commercial/investment bank separation); 2000 Commodity Futures Modernization Act deregulating credit default swaps (CDS).
- **Leverage build-up**: 2004 SEC relaxation of the 12:1 net capital rule let investment banks (Bear Stearns, Lehman, Merrill Lynch, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley) leverage up to ~30x.
- **Sub-prime lending mechanics**: predatory lending to low-credit-score (FICO) borrowers at above-prime rates.
- **Risk indicators**: TED spread, Baa spread, VIX, and MOVE index as gauges of credit/market stress.
- **Securitization chain**: mortgages bundled into MBS/CDOs, risk passed from banks to hedge funds to investors, "insured" via CDS; Asset-Backed Commercial Paper (ABCP) used to fund illiquid assets short-term — froze in Aug 2007.
- **Crisis timeline**: New Century Financial collapse (Apr 2007) → Northern Rock run → Bear Stearns rescue (Mar 2008) → Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac seizure and Lehman Brothers bankruptcy (Sept 2008) → AIG bailout → Washington Mutual failure → global stock market crashes and central bank liquidity injections/rate cuts.
- **Policy response**: TARP ($700bn, later $475bn), bank equity injections, and the Dodd-Frank Act (2010) — FSOC, Orderly Liquidation Authority, CFPB, Volcker Rule, derivatives reform, stress tests.
- **Lessons**: housing bubbles need tracking; weak regulation (not just liquidity shortage) causes crises; "Too Big to Fail" and misaligned incentives must be addressed; need for global coordination and stronger LOLR/bailout frameworks.

## Why This Matters

This is predominantly **conceptual/narrative** — a historical chronology of institutional failures, regulatory decisions, and policy responses — rather than quantitative modeling. A few technical terms appear (leverage ratios, TED/Baa spreads, VIX, MBS/CDO/CDS/ABCP mechanics) that go beyond basic macro (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, forex reserves), but they are explained in plain language in the slides themselves. Someone with only basic macro background should find it **accessible with light extra effort** on the derivatives/securitization jargon; no formal modeling is required.

## Relevance to the Role-Play Simulation

**Medium.** It gives essential historical grounding — how banking-sector risk builds, how governments/central banks intervene (LOLR, bailouts, TARP), and the regulatory aftermath (Dodd-Frank) — useful background for Fed Chair, RBI Governor/Deputies, and Finance Ministry roles, but it is entirely US/UK-centric with no India-specific balance-of-payments, forex-reserve, or IMF-program mechanics, so it won't directly equip roles like IMF Mission Chief or the India-side policy roles with decision-specific tools for the simulation.
