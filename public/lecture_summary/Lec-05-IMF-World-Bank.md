# Lecture 5: International Financial Architecture — IMF & The World Bank

## Key Concepts

- **International Financial Architecture (IFA):** the network of multilateral institutions (IMF, World Bank, BIS, FSB, G7/G20, MDBs, IOSCO, IAIS) that safeguard global monetary/financial stability; roles overlap in places (financial stability) and have gaps (e.g., no dedicated climate-finance body).
- **International Lender of Last Resort (ILLR) debate:** whether IMF should/can act like a domestic central bank's LOLR for cross-border crises; tension between crisis-bailout capacity, national sovereignty, and moral hazard.
- **Bretton Woods (1944):** birthed IMF and IBRD (precursor to World Bank); Harry Dexter White (US) and John Maynard Keynes (UK) led the respective commissions.
- **World Bank Group** = IBRD (market-rate loans to middle-income countries) + IDA (concessional loans/grants to poorest countries) + IFC (private-sector investment) + MIGA (political-risk insurance) + ICSID (investment-dispute arbitration). India is a founding member of IBRD/IDA/IFC but joined MIGA late (1994) and has never joined ICSID.
- **IMF core functions:** lending, surveillance (Article IV consultations, FSAP financial-sector assessments), and capacity development/technical assistance.
- **IMF governance:** quota-based system (SDR-denominated); quotas set voting power, financing access, and SDR allocations. General Quota Reviews (GRQs) periodically resize/reallocate quotas; US holds an effective veto (>15% needed to block 85%-majority decisions). India's Article IV (2025) and FSAP (2025) findings were covered as a live example.
- **Capital account convertibility (Article VIII):** not mandated by IMF Articles; approach is case-by-case, sequenced liberalization — reinforced after the IMF backed off pushing full CAC following the 1997–98 Asian Financial Crisis.
- **IMF Lending Facilities** — distinguished by funding source (GRA = non-concessional general resources; PRGT = concessional, for low-income countries; RST = resilience/sustainability trust), purpose/urgency, duration, and whether conditionality is ex-ante (qualification-based, for precautionary/strong performers) or ex-post (attached after disbursement, for actual crisis use):
  - **SBA** (Stand-By Arrangement): GRA, actual/potential BoP need, ~12–18 months (up to 3 yrs), ex-post conditionality — the classic crisis-lending tool.
  - **EFF** (Extended Fund Facility): GRA, protracted BoP needs requiring structural reform, up to 4 years.
  - **ECF** (Extended Credit Facility): PRGT (concessional) equivalent of EFF for low-income countries, 3–5 years.
  - **SCF** (Standby Credit Facility): PRGT equivalent of SBA, 1–3 years, moderate needs.
  - **RFI** (Rapid Financing Instrument): GRA, urgent/emergency need, outright purchase, no ongoing conditionality.
  - **RCF** (Rapid Credit Facility): PRGT equivalent of RFI for low-income emergency financing.
  - **FCL** (Flexible Credit Line): GRA, purely precautionary, ex-ante qualification only, for countries with very strong fundamentals (no stigma of a "crisis" program).
  - **SLL** (Short-term Liquidity Line): GRA, revolving 12-month precautionary line for moderate BoP pressure from capital-account volatility.
  - **PLL** (Precautionary Liquidity Line): GRA, 6-month to 2-year, ex-ante qualification plus some ex-post conditionality — sits between SBA and FCL for sound-but-imperfect economies.
- **IMF stigma:** borrowing carries political/market reputational costs, debated as genuine stigma vs. adverse selection/moral hazard (recidivism).

## Why This Matters
This lecture is mostly **conceptual/institutional**, not quantitative — governance structures, mandates, history, and a taxonomy of lending instruments, with only light math (the quota formula). A student with only basic macro background (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, forex reserves) should find it accessible; the main new vocabulary is institutional (quotas, GRA/PRGT, Article IV/VIII) rather than technical modeling.

## Relevance to the Crisis Simulation
**High.** The lending-facility taxonomy, conditionality/stigma dynamics, and Article IV/FSAP surveillance process are exactly the tools an "IMF Mission Chief/Deputy/ED" would invoke, and the sovereignty-vs-conditionality tension directly shapes how RBI/Finance Ministry roles would negotiate with the IMF during a simulated BoP crisis.
