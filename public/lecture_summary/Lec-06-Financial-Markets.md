# Lecture 6: Financial Markets — Money Markets, Bond/Debt Markets & FX Markets (Mridul Saggar, IIM Kozhikode)

## Key Concepts Covered

- **Money markets in India**: uncollateralized call/notice/term money; repo, CROMS, TREPS (successor to CBLO); CPs, CDs, T-bills, ABCPs (not permitted in India); Money Market Mutual Funds. Policy repo rate anchors money market rates within the SDF–MSF interest rate corridor. Money markets act as an early-warning "plumbing" indicator of systemic stress (rate spikes, widening spreads, flight to safety, standing-facility usage).
- **Bond/debt market basics**: par value, coupon, YTM, price-yield inverse relationship, duration, convexity, coupon structures (fixed, floating, step-up, credit-linked, PIK, zero-coupon, perpetual), credit ratings (PD/LGD, national vs. global rating scales), green/blue/yellow/transition bonds.
- **Corporate financing theory**: Modigliani-Miller I/II, pecking-order theory (Myers-Majluf), financing-constraints literature (Fazzari et al.), and Indian corporates' shift toward internal financing.
- **G-sec market**: institutional structure (RBI as debt manager, IDMD, PDO, CCIL), auction mechanics, Primary Dealer system, fiscal deficit/public debt trends (Centre and states), debt sustainability, ownership pattern shifts, FPI routes (FAR/VRR), G-sec risks (market, reinvestment, liquidity), RBI's failed soft yield-curve-control attempt during Covid.
- **FX markets**: OTC structure and turnover ($7.5tn/day), FX swaps/spot dominance, USD's ~88-89% share of turnover, Global FX Code, BIS's role (BCBS, CGFS, Markets Committee, CPMI, FSB), algo/HFT trading, carry trades (2024 unwind episode), cross-currency basis trades, de-dollarization of reserves (~70%→57%) vs. persistent dollar dominance in trade invoicing, swap lines and Regional Financing Arrangements (GFSN), Triffin Dilemma, Exorbitant Privilege, and Special Drawing Rights (SDRs).

## Why This Matters (Accessibility)

This is predominantly **technical/institutional content** — heavy on market mechanics, instrument taxonomies, pricing formulas (YTM, duration, convexity) and India-specific regulatory plumbing (SEBI/RBI mandates, CCIL, CROMS). A student with only basic macro background (money supply, exchange rates, inflation, forex reserves) would find the bond-pricing and market-microstructure sections a real stretch, since they assume prior finance coursework. However, the FX/reserve-currency material (de-dollarization, Triffin Dilemma, swap lines, carry trades) connects naturally to macro/forex-reserve intuition and is more accessible narrative content.

## Relevance to the Financial Crisis Role-Play Simulation: **High**

The lecture directly supplies the operational toolkit a crisis simulation would need — RBI liquidity facilities and money-market stress signals, G-sec/debt-sustainability dynamics relevant to the Finance Ministry, dollar-shortage/swap-line/GFSN mechanics relevant to the IMF and Fed roles, and carry-trade unwind dynamics that could plausibly trigger or amplify a simulated crisis — making it strong background material for RBI Governor/Deputy Governor, Finance Secretary, IMF Mission, and Fed Chair roles.
