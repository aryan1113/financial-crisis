FINANCIAL CRISES COURSE — PREREQUISITE ASSESSMENT & STUDY PLAN
================================================================

Background considered:
- Data scientist, no formal finance education
- ~50% through a basic macroeconomics book (money supply, exchange rates,
  inflation, forex reserves)
- 10/60 videos into an NPTEL "International Finance Management" course
- Undecided on CFA/FRM, "bleak interest" — testing the subject out

IS THIS COURSE RIGHT AS A FIRST FINANCE COURSE?
------------------------------------------------
Usable, but not zero-prerequisite. It's built for PGP (MBA-equivalent)
students who've already done a core macro/finance sequence.

Assessed against Lectures 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9:

Where background is enough:
- Lec-4 (Wars & Markets): mostly historical/narrative, light econ overlay. Fine.
- Lec-5 (IMF/World Bank): institutional detail, self-contained, low math. Fine.
- Lec-3 (Macro aggregates & BoP): closest overlap with existing background
  (money supply, BoP, forex reserves); goes one level deeper (BPM6
  classification, Marshall-Lerner, Domar condition).
- Lec-9 (Historical crises): narrative-driven, accessible, though the
  speculative-attack models (Krugman/Obstfeld) assume some monetary theory.

Where real gaps exist:
- Lec-2 (Monetary Policy): assumes comfort with interest-rate corridor
  mechanics and time-inconsistency/credibility game theory
  (Kydland-Prescott, Rogoff, Barro-Gordon). Not in a basic macro book.
- Lec-6 (Financial Markets) — BIGGEST GAP: assumes bond math (coupon, YTM,
  duration, convexity) and capital structure theory (Modigliani-Miller,
  pecking order). This is corporate-finance-101 material, not macro.

RECOMMENDATION
--------------
No need for CFA or FRM — overkill for casual/curious interest. Two specific
building blocks are missing, not a whole finance degree:

1. Finish the basic macro book (currently at 50%). The second half likely
   covers fiscal policy, IS-LM/AD-AS, and BoP mechanics feeding into Lec-2
   and Lec-3.

2. Do a short, targeted bond/fixed-income primer — just coupon, yield,
   duration, and why bond prices move opposite to yields. Unlocks Lec-6 and
   much of Lec-9's crisis mechanics (bond sell-offs, spreads). No need for
   the full CFA Level 1 curriculum — a few chapters of a fixed-income intro
   (e.g. Bodie/Kane/Marcus "Investments," bond pricing chapter, or free
   resources like Investopedia's bond basics) is enough.

Skip standalone monetary-theory/game-theory prep (time-inconsistency models)
— rabbit hole for a "curious, not committed" learner. Let the lecture
context carry it; look up Kydland-Prescott/Rogoff only if it doesn't click.

SUGGESTED FLOW
--------------
1. Finish the macro book.
2. Spend a weekend on bond basics.
3. Keep going with the NPTEL IFM course in parallel — directly supports
   Lec-5/Lec-9 exchange-rate content.
4. Take this course's lectures roughly in order; slow down on Lec-6.
