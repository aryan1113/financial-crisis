# Crisis Simulation Cheat Sheet

One page to skim right before the roleplay. Pulled from Tier 1 + Tier 2 lecture summaries.

## 1. Identify the crisis type fast (first 2 minutes)

| Type | Trigger | Symptom | Endgame |
|---|---|---|---|
| Currency crisis | Peg misalignment, capital flight, reserve depletion | Currency in free fall, central bank burning reserves | Peg abandoned or IMF called |
| Banking crisis | Panic, bank run, NPAs | Depositors queuing, a bank/NBFC fails | LOLR support, bailout, nationalization |
| Sovereign debt crisis | Unsustainable public debt | Bond yields spike, downgrade, can't roll over | Restructuring, haircut, IMF programme |

Real-world templates to borrow from: **Argentina** (peg defense → devaluation → IMF), **Asian Financial Crisis** (currency crisis cascading into banking crisis), **Taper Tantrum** (Fed shock hits India specifically — closest India precedent), **Tequila/ERM** (classic peg-defense-fails-to-speculators narrative), **COVID** (shock-driven, needs coordinated fiscal+monetary response).

## 2. IMF instrument cheat-sheet ("what instrument was used")

| Situation | Facility | One-liner |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency, need cash now, no time to negotiate | **RFI** | Outright disbursement, no ongoing conditionality reviews |
| Standard ongoing BoP problem | **SBA** | Up to 3 yrs, ex-post conditionality — the "default" IMF loan |
| Deep structural problem, not just a cash crunch | **EFF** | Up to 4 yrs, tied to structural reforms |
| Country not in crisis, wants insurance | **FCL** | Ex-ante qualification only, for strong-fundamentals countries — no stigma |
| Poor-country equivalents | **ECF / SCF / RCF** | Same logic as EFF/SBA/RFI but concessional (PRGT-funded) |

Which facility is chosen is itself a story: RFI/RCF = "urgent, no time for a program." SBA/EFF = "negotiated, conditional — watch for austerity backlash." FCL = "we're fine, just a signal" — good gotcha if the government is downplaying severity while quietly negotiating one.

## 3. Early warning indicators to cite as evidence

- FX reserves level and trend (months of import cover)
- Credit growth vs. long-term trend (BIS credit-to-GDP gap)
- Money-market stress — entities trading off-market rates (Saggar's own preferred single best banking-crisis signal)
- Real exchange rate deviation, M2/reserves ratio, short-term external debt, current account deficit
- Fiscal deficit and public debt trajectory

Use these to answer "didn't you see this coming?" — the honest answer is usually "yes, but acting earlier has its own political cost."

## 4. RBI's actual policy toolkit (what the central bank can announce)

- **Rate tools**: repo rate hikes/cuts, CRR/SLR changes, MSF-repo-SDF corridor
- **Liquidity tools**: OMOs, LTROs/TLTROs, G-SAP, Standing Liquidity Facility
- **FX defense**: spot/forward intervention (state a number — "we've deployed $X bn"), swap lines, FCNR(B)-style deposit schemes to pull in dollar inflows
- **Unconventional (UMPTs)**: QE, forward guidance, negative rates, yield curve control (RBI tried this in COVID and it partly failed — good realism detail)
- **Capital flow management**: capital controls (Malaysia 1998 precedent), import duty hikes on gold, incentive schemes for overseas borrowing

## 5. Crisis communication rules (the actual press-conference script)

- Speak with **one voice** — no daylight between RBI, Finance Ministry, IMF statements (if there is daylight, that's the press's best cross-question)
- State plainly **who is affected and what's being done** — always answer "is my money safe" directly
- **Never state a problem without a solution** attached in the same breath
- **Don't over-promise** — absolute guarantees get falsified by the next headline
- Deliver bad news definitively, without alarm — hedging reads as panic
- Reference case study: Draghi's "whatever it takes" (2012) — one credible sentence compressed peripheral bond spreads from 500+ bps to 100-200bps

## 6. Vocabulary to drop naturally

BoP crisis, capital flight, contagion, sovereign spread, credit rating downgrade, conditionality, Letter of Intent (LoI), haircut, moral hazard, LOLR, "original sin" (EM can't borrow abroad in own currency), stigma (of IMF borrowing), sudden stop, twin deficits, fragile five.

## 7. Press-specific playbook (round-table, not 1:1 interviews)

1. Central bank announces its response first — **listen for a contradiction or evasion**, then cross-question it live (this is what impressed Prof. Saggar most in a prior batch's run).
2. Divide angles across your press group beforehand: one on reserves/numbers, one on accountability ("why didn't you act sooner"), one on political fallout, one on citizen impact.
3. Best killer questions:
   - "Has the government approached the IMF? If not, at what point does the delay itself become the story?"
   - "You said reserves are adequate, but you're intervening daily — which is it?"
   - "Is this heading toward an RFI given the urgency, or a full SBA program?"
   - "The Finance Minister said X, but you just said Y — which of you is right?"
